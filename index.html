PAVE: Preference-Aligned Visual Egocentric Learning for the Blind and Low-Vision Users

Nan Li, Shaofeng Liang, Shiyun Zhao, et al.

ManuscriptUnder review · WWW 2027

Turning visual correctness into useful assistance. PAVE connects BLV-informed supervision, question-conditioned response policies, and correctness-first post-training.

Keywords: Egocentric VQA; Multimodal Learning; Preference Alignment; Assistive AI

Abstract

Egocentric video question answering enables blind and low-vision (BLV) users to query their immediate surroundings through natural language. However, existing benchmarks and models primarily optimize for semantic agreement with reference answers, reducing visual assistance to a purely factual matching task. In real-world scenarios, a factually correct response remains practically unhelpful if it lacks the spatial orientation, actionable detail, or conciseness required by the immediate task of the user. Drawing upon real BLV egocentric videos and authentic user queries from EgoBlind, we construct the PA-EgoQA, a preference-aligned dataset that reconstructs reference answers according to empirical BLV communication criteria. Based on this dataset, we introduce Preference-Aligned Visual Egocentric Learning (PAVE), a unified learning and inference framework that couples structured answer-policy fine-tuning with deterministic question-conditioned routing to adapt response formulation directly to the surface form of the query at inference time. We further align the model via FILTER-GRPO, which pairs a hierarchical correctness-quality reward with contrast-based candidate-group filtering to extract learning signals from both factual accuracy contrasts and fine-grained quality variations among correct answers. Extensive experiments across three vision-language backbones demonstrate consistent improvements in factual accuracy alongside substantial gains in assistive response quality. These results establish a concrete paradigm for translating human-centered response requirements into egocentric supervision, generation policies, and post-training objectives.

ReRoute · Overview & analysisClick to enlarge

One Direction, Many Routes: Uncovering a Shared Adversarial Vulnerability in Dynamic-Depth Language Models

Shiyun Zhao*, Shaofeng Liang*, Nan Li*, et al.

ManuscriptUnder review · WWW 2027

Investigating what adaptive computation shares. ReRoute uses routing-derived supervision to study shared adversarial vulnerabilities across dynamic-depth language models.

Keywords: Dynamic-Depth LLMs; Adversarial Robustness; Routing; Transferability

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) power a wide range of Web services, from search summarization to autonomous Web agents. To reduce the high computational cost of serving these models, dynamic-depth inference has emerged as an effective paradigm. Its token representations dynamically determine which layers to execute. Consequently, each representation serves a dual role: it encodes semantic content while simultaneously governing subsequent computation. In this work, we explore whether this mechanism could introduce a transferable vulnerability. Our key insight is that the routing decisions produced by a model on clean text provide intrinsic, label-free supervision. These decisions exist for any arbitrary input, require no task labels, and indicate on which side of each decision boundary the input currently resides. Based on this observation, we propose REROUTE that learns embedding perturbations to invert clean routing decisions while preserving language modeling fidelity along the original route. It also distills these perturbations into discrete token substitutions. REROUTE reduces the zero-shot accuracy of the source model by 42.9% and transfers across unseen routing architectures with relative accuracy drops of 33.3% to 35.3%. Moreover, the learned perturbations collapse onto a single dominant direction in the input embedding space. Our theoretical and causal analyses show that this shared direction emerges from aligned router-boundary pullbacks, simultaneously corrupting backbone representations and amplifying performance degradation through discrete rerouting.

ObsBridge: Observation-Grounded Conditioning for Ego Trajectory Prediction

Zhenhao Wang*, Shaofeng Liang*, Jiemin Wu, Sijia Chen, Keni Shi, Nan Li, et al.

ManuscriptUnder review · CVPR 2027

ObsBridge connects recovered motion histories with observation evidence for robust pedestrian trajectory forecasting from ego-view inputs.

Keywords: Ego Trajectory Prediction; Motion History Recovery; Observation Grounding; Flow Matching

Abstract

Pedestrian forecasting from ego-view observations is challenging because occlusion, limited visibility, and tracking errors corrupt observed motion histories. Such errors are particularly consequential for autonomous robot navigation. Existing paradigms typically either impute noisy history positions before forecasting or jointly train an auxiliary history reconstruction branch. However, directly feeding imperfect historical estimates into a predictor causes a severe distribution shift between training and deployment. Meanwhile, auxiliary reconstruction still computes inter-agent interactions over raw, corrupted coordinates. Our key insight is that robust forecasting under ego-view noise requires explicitly coupling recovered historical states with raw observation evidence to gradually adapt the predictor and calibrate inter-agent relations. Building on this insight, we propose ObsBridge, a flow-based trajectory forecasting framework with observation-grounded historical bridging. Specifically, ObsBridge first employs a frozen estimator to recover historical motion from agent-relative and ego-relative positions, image-plane locations, and visibility states. During training, we introduce an agent-wise curriculum that gradually replaces clean teacher histories with complete estimated histories before context encoding. This allows the predictor to smoothly adapt to residual recovery errors. Furthermore, we design a residual geometry adapter to fuse the encoded motion with original observation evidence. We aggregate the resulting representations through social attention to condition the flow-based trajectory generator. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that ObsBridge consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods. On EgoTraj-TBD, it reduces minADE@20 by 5.81% and minFDE@20 by 9.15%.

DRMOT: A Dataset and Framework for RGBD Referring Multi-Object Tracking

Sijia Chen*, Shaofeng Liang*, Lijuan Ma*, Yanqiu Yu, Nan Li, et al.

ManuscriptUnder review · IEEE TCSVT

Bringing depth into language-guided tracking. DRSet connects RGB-D observations with referring expressions, supporting research on depth-aware spatial grounding and trajectory association.

Keywords: RGB-D; Referring Multi-Object Tracking; Spatial Grounding; Multimodal Perception

Abstract

Referring Multi-Object Tracking (RMOT) aims to track specific targets based on language descriptions, playing a vital role in interactive AI systems such as robotics and autonomous driving. However, existing RMOT methods rely exclusively on 2D RGB observations. Since similar 2D projections can correspond to different 3D configurations, the available observations may not uniquely determine the referred targets. This creates a fundamental mismatch between the task requirements and the available input. To advance research on the DRMOT task, we construct DRSet, a dataset tailored for the DRMOT task. DRSet comprises 2,076 referring expressions over 186 RGB-D source sequences. It features synchronized RGB images and depth maps paired with language descriptions, including explicit depth-dependent descriptions, to systematically evaluate spatial-semantic detection and tracking capabilities. Furthermore, we propose DRTrack, an MLLM-guided depth-referring tracking framework. DRTrack leverages a Geometric-Aware GRPO fine-tuned Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to achieve precise spatial-semantic detection from joint L+RGB+D inputs, while incorporating explicit depth cues to enforce robust trajectory association. Extensive experiments on the DRSet dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework.

All-Optical Neuromorphic Logic Processing Units Using Monolithic Black Phosphorus

Huide Wang*, Yi Liu*, Wenkai Wang*, Ziqian Wang, Yihan Zhu, Yanqi Ge, Songrui Wei, Bowen Du, Meng Qiu, Nan Li, et al.

ManuscriptUnder review · Science Advances

I developed and trained the differentiable logic-gate SNN component for this collaborative black-phosphorus photonic computing study, including the soft-to-hard Boolean-gate conversion and evaluation pipeline.

Keywords: Photonic Computing; Neuromorphic Logic; Black Phosphorus; Spiking Neural Networks

Abstract

The neuromorphic logic processing unit (nLPU) represents a significant evolutionary leap beyond conventional logic processing units (LPUs), offering a highly promising pathway to break through the von Neumann bottleneck via high-throughput, energy-efficient brain-inspired computing. However, achieving multifunctional and reconfigurable nLPUs within a single hardware platform remains a fundamental challenge. Here, we demonstrate a reconfigurable photonic nLPU computing architecture based on a single-layer black phosphorus (BP) van der Waals heterostructure. By integrating the anisotropic photoluminescence (PL) of BP with nonvolatile optically controlled ferroelectric modulation, this platform seamlessly achieves dynamic reconfigurable switching among seven fundamental Boolean logic operations (AND, OR, NOT, NOR, NAND, XOR, XNOR), while being compatible with both the continuous-time driving and discrete sparse spike event-driven mechanisms underpinning spiking neural networks (SNNs). By combining these physical logic primitives with a differentiable training strategy, our photonic nLPU architecture exhibits high-accuracy parallel pattern recognition capability, with substantially reduced computational latency and power consumption. This work bridges the gap between physical optical computing and neuro-symbolic logic architectures, laying the foundation for the development of next-generation intelligent photonic processors.

Beyond Glare: Reliability-Aware Burst Restoration for Robust Facial Expression Recognition

BERR · Reliability-aware four-frame restoration

BERR uses lightweight four-frame restoration to recover facial cues obscured by eyeglass glare for robust expression recognition.

Keywords: Burst Restoration; Glare Removal; Reliability-Aware Fusion; Facial Expression Recognition

Abstract

Eyeglass reflections obscure facial cues needed for reliable expression recognition in AI-assisted learning. BERR is a lightweight four-frame reflection-removal network that combines complementary observations through degradation-aware temporal fusion. We construct LensGlare-4 with 2,243 synthetic glare bursts and integrate the BERR–FER pipeline into an undergraduate physics tutoring platform. Under strong glare on FER2013, BERR improves accuracy by 0.54 percentage points and Macro-F1 by 0.78 percentage points. The resulting visual front end supports multimodal student-state perception and personalized teaching.

RecurBool: Boolean Circuit Compilation Toward Photonic Recurrent Computing

Compiling recurrent language models into fixed Boolean circuits as a verifiable foundation for future photonic recurrent computing.

Keywords: Recurrent Neural Networks; Boolean Logic Circuits; Precision-Aware Compilation; Low-Bit Quantization; Photonic Computing

Abstract

This study explores the conversion of recurrent neural networks into executable Boolean logic circuits and presents RecurBool, a precision-aware circuit compilation framework for recurrent language models. It aims to provide a verifiable logic representation for future photonic computing architectures with state memory and temporal processing capabilities. Starting from a trained and frozen gated recurrent unit (GRU), the framework uses grouped quantization, per-dimension codebook calibration, and quantization-error compensation to reduce numerical precision while preserving as much of the original model’s predictive performance as possible. Using 5-bit weight and recurrent-state encodings, RecurBool converts gating computations, nonlinear mappings, arithmetic operations, and state updates into two-input Boolean circuits with fixed gate types and connectivity. The compiled circuit preserves the GRU’s gated memory mechanism, is reused across time steps, and predicts successive characters through autonomous state feedback without relying on the original floating-point model. On the full text8 validation set, RecurBool achieves 1.7436 BPC, close to the original FP32 GRU’s 1.7116 BPC; on byte-level WikiText-2 validation, it achieves 1.9165 BPB, close to the original model’s 1.8903 BPB. The circuit implementation also passes code-by-code consistency checks on random inputs and long-sequence prefixes, establishing agreement with the corresponding finite-precision reference computation. These results demonstrate the feasibility of converting recurrent language models into fixed Boolean logic circuits and provide a methodological foundation for further research into circuit optimization, hardware-aware compilation, and recurrent computation mapping for photonic devices.

Carrier-Mediated All-Optical Channel Attention for Diffractive Neural Computing

Input-adaptive optical feature recalibration

Investigating input-adaptive optical feature recalibration through diffractive computation and VCSOA cross-gain modulation. I conceived the architecture and developed its preliminary feasibility plan.

Keywords: Diffractive Neural Computing; Optical Channel Attention; Carrier Dynamics; Cross-Gain Modulation

Abstract

Diffractive optical neural networks process two-dimensional optical fields in a spatially parallel manner, yet their fixed optical transformations cannot readily provide input-dependent channel recalibration. We propose a carrier-mediated all-optical channel attention framework that introduces input-dependent multiplicative modulation into two-dimensional optical features while keeping the trained diffractive parameters fixed. The key idea is to use input-generated control light to regulate the carrier state of a semiconductor amplifier, embedding dynamic channel-weight generation into light–matter interactions. Specifically, an optical attention branch generates control light through feature compression and cross-channel diffractive mixing, then adjusts the gain of each feature channel in the main branch through cross-gain modulation in a broad-area vertical-cavity semiconductor optical amplifier (VCSOA). Under weak-signal and approximately spatially uniform operating conditions, this mechanism applies input-dependent gain to the complete two-dimensional feature field to achieve channel recalibration. We further design a device-calibration-driven joint optimization procedure that incorporates device-response constraints into the co-design of front-end feature encoding, attention mapping, and back-end diffractive readout. By combining learnable static optical mappings with input-driven dynamic gain, the framework provides a design for optical channel attention without per-sample electronic weight computation or modulator refresh.
